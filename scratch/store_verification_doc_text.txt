1. Introduction
Effective inventory management is a critical component of any organization that 
handles physical goods. Accurate stock records, controlled storage practices, 
and efficient monitoring processes are essential to ensure operational continuity, 
financial accuracy, and accountability within the supply chain. As businesses grow 
and warehouse operations become more complex, the need for a structured and 
technology-driven stock verification system becomes increasingly important.
 
This document presents an overview of the Store-Verification-Process-and-Stock-
Control Framework, outlining the current operational practices and the proposed 
improvements aimed at enhancing accuracy, transparency, and control within the 
inventory management process. The objective of this document is to establish a 
clear and standardized methodology for conducting stock verification, managing 
warehouse locations, handling stock transfers, and maintaining proper records of 
damaged inventory.
The document explains the existing-store-verification-process and introduces 
improved procedures such as daily- stock- verification- with- controlled- day-
closing,-periodic-stock-checks-based-on-defined-frequencies,-and-automated-
variance detection to identify discrepancies between system records and physical 
inventory. It also incorporates a Damaged-Item-Management-process, ensuring 
that unusable or defective items are properly recorded, segregated, and considered 
during stock verification to maintain accurate inventory reporting.
In addition, a s tandardized-Rack,-Bin,-and-Location-Numbering-System is 
proposed to improve warehouse organization and ensure that inventory items can be 
easily located, tracked, and verified. The document also defines a Stock Transfer 
Management framework , outlining procedures, transfer scenarios, and status 
lifecycle controls to ensure that stock movements between locations are properly 
recorded and approved.
Management review points are included throughout the document to support 
oversight, monitor operational performance, and facilitate continuous improvements 
in warehouse and inventory management practices.
Overall, this document establishes a- structured,- transparent,- and- scalable-
inventory-control-framework-designed to improve operational efficiency, minimize 
discrepancies, and strengthen accountability within the store management system.

2. Store Verification Process
2.1. Objective
The Stock Verification Process defines the standardized procedure for validating incoming inventory 
against approved purchase documents before items are accepted into store inventory. The purpose of 
this process is to ensure accuracy in quantity verification, batch identification, labelling, and system 
registration while maintaining a clear audit trail.
This process establishes controlled coordination between the purchase team and store operations to 
eliminate manual inconsistencies, prevent unauthorized stock entry, and improve traceability of goods 
from vendor receipt to final storage. By implementing a structured verification workflow, the organization 
ensures that all items entering the inventory are properly documented, labelled, and approved in 
accordance with operational and compliance standards.
The proposed system introduces automated batch tracking, role-based approvals, and barcode/QR-
enabled identification to support efficient inventory management and real-time stock visibility across 
branches.
2.2. -Current-Process-Overview
In the existing system, the verification workflow is partially manual and distributed between the 
purchase and store teams.
2.2.1. Invoice-Based-PVN-Generation
 • When the purchase team receives a vendor invoice, it is manually compared with the purchase 
order.
 • A Purchase Verification Note (PVN) is generated and forwarded to the main store.
 • If the invoice arrives with the shipment, the store supervisor generates the PVN.
2.2.2. Physical-Verification
 • The store supervisor compares the PVN against physically received stock.
 • PVN contains item details and quantity only; pricing is excluded.
 • Quantity received is recorded manually.
 • Barcode stickers are generated manually based on received quantity.
2.2.3. 1.2.3-Batch-&-Labelling-Structure
 • A Batch code id maintained in the format: VendorCode/MM/YY
 • Labels contain:
 • PCode
 • Barcode
 • MRP
2.2.4.  Handling Unregistered Items
 •  Items not registered in the system are marked as non-PCode.
 • Items are set aside.

2.3.2. -Store-Level-Verification
Physical stock verification is conducted after PVN creation to ensure accuracy between documentation 
and received goods.
Process-Steps
 • Store Supervisor matches PVN items with physical stock.
 • Received quantity is updated item-wise.
 • Required number of labels/stickers is entered item-wise.
 • System automatically triggers barcode or QR label printing based on confirmed print quantity.
Once the PVN (Purchase Voucher Note) is generated by the Purchase Department, the system will 
automatically push the PVN list to the Main Store Manager. Upon receiving it, the Store Manager, along 
with the Supervisors and Storekeepers, will physically verify the items against the PVN.
For example, if an item with OEM number 100 was ordered with a quantity of 10, and the same quantity 
with the correct brand is physically received, the Store Manager will have the facility on their tablet device 
to generate a QR code or barcode containing the PCODE, date of arrival, and other relevant details.
Once generated, the connected Bluetooth printer will print the required number of barcode stickers 
as specified by the Store Manager. These stickers will then be affixed to the respective items by the 
Storekeepers.
If a Storekeeper has any doubts regarding a sticker, they can use their mobile device to scan the QR 
code or barcode. Upon scanning, the system will display all relevant information except pricing and other 
confidential data. The displayed information will 
include:
 • OEM Number
 • Part Number
 • Brand
 • Rack Number
 • Bin Number / Location Number in the store
Based on this information, the Storekeeper can correctly place the item in the designated rack or bin 
location. Alternatively, items may be temporarily placed and later moved to the appropriate location as 
required.
In addition to this process, the Store Manager will have the ability to update the received quantities in 
the PVN within the system. After completing the physical verification and updating all received items, 
the Store Manager can formally submit the PVN back to the Purchase Department for further processing 
and confirmation.
Expected-Outcome
Inventory accuracy is validated at the entry point.

2.3.3. -Batch-Code-Standardization
A standardized batch code format is implemented to enhance traceability and audit readiness.
Batch Format: VendorCode / YY / MM  (Existing Pattern)
Batch Format: PVN/ YY / MM / DD (Proposed Pattern)
The benefit of using the new-Batch-Code-method-in the QR/Barcode sticker system is that it enables 
complete traceability of each item.
When the PVN number is embedded within the sticker, the system can retrieve all relevant details of 
that specific item, including:
 • Date of arrival
 • Invoice number
 • Purchase Order (PO) details
 • Brand information
 • Batch information
 • Other related item details
This ensures accurate tracking and easy verification at any stage within the store.
The barcode is especially useful for sales operations. During billing, the barcode can be scanned to 
quickly add the item to the invoice, ensuring accuracy and faster checkout. The system will automatically 
fetch the required item details and generate the bill accordingly.
Business Benefits
The implementation of standardized batch management delivers the following measurable business 
advantages:
 • Unique- Batch- Identification: Ensures every delivery is assigned a distinct batch reference, 
eliminating duplication and reducing the risk of stock misidentification.
 • Clear-Separation-of-Deliveries: Enables accurate differentiation between multiple deliveries within 
the same time period, preventing inventory mixing and improving operational clarity.
 • Improved-Tracking-and-Reporting: Enhances end-to-end traceability of inventory, allowing faster 
retrieval of batch history and more reliable operational and financial reporting.
 • Stronger-Audit-and-Compliance-Control: Establishes a structured audit trail that supports internal 
controls, regulatory requirements, and inspection readiness.
 • Reduced-Operational-Errors: Minimizes manual tracking mistakes through system-driven batch 
standardization.
 • Faster-Inventory-Reconciliation: Speeds up stock verification and reconciliation activities during 
audits and physical counts.

Overview
The system enforces a structured, multi-level approval workflow to ensure that all stock entries are 
formally validated before being released into the main inventory. This workflow establishes accountability, 
prevents unauthorized stock movement, and maintains a complete audit trail.
Workflow-Sequence

2. Routing-to-Purchase-Team: The verified PVN is automatically routed to the Purchase Team for

This workflow ensures operational accuracy, accountability, and a complete audit trail.

3. STOCK VERIFICATION PROCESS
(System-Driven Mandatory Sampling Model – 30% to 50%)
3.1. DAILY-STOCK-VERIFICATION-&-CONTROLLED-DAY-CLOSING
(Mandatory Algorithm-Based Verification Before Next-Day Operations)
Objective
To ensure that daily stock accuracy is maintained by enforcing a system-controlled verification process 
based on daily sales activity and fast-moving weighted selection.
The system will not allow the start of the next business day unless daily stock verification is completed 
and approved.
This ensures continuous operational discipline and real-time inventory accuracy.
Core-Concept
At the end of each business day:
 • The system analyzes that day’s sales transactions.
 • Based on an intelligent selection algorithm,
 • 30% of the items sold that day (weighted by movement and risk) must be physically verified.
 • Verification must be completed and approved by:
 • Storekeeper
 • Store Manager
 • Only after verification and confirmation can the system close the day.
If the process is not completed:
 • New day transactions cannot begin.
 • Inter-branch transfers will be blocked.
 • Stock issue functions will be disabled.
