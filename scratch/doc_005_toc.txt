PREFACE
In today’s dynamic business environment, organizations require a robust and integrated
financial management system to ensure accuracy, transparency, compliance, and operational
efficiency. Effective financial control is no longer limited to recording transactions; it extends to
real-time monitoring of assets, liabilities, inventory, cash flow, budgeting, taxation, and branch-
level operations across the enterprise.
This document has been prepared as a comprehensive guide to the Accounting and Financial
Management framework within the ERP system. It outlines the financial processes, operational
workflows, controls, and implementation standards required to establish a consistent and scalable
financial ecosystem for organizations operating across multiple branches and business units.
The contents of this document cover the complete financial lifecycle, beginning with the Chart
of Accounts and General Ledger structure, followed by Accounts Payable, Accounts Receivable,
Inventory-Integrated Accounting, Banking and Treasury Management, Budgeting, Multi-Currency
Management, Tax Compliance, and Financial Reporting. The document also provides a structured
guideline for new branch implementation, ensuring standardized ERP deployment and financial
governance across all locations.
The primary objectives of this document are to:
• Establish a standardized accounting structure across the organization.
• Ensure accurate financial recording and reporting in compliance with accounting standards
and statutory requirements.
• Integrate inventory, procurement, sales, and financial transactions into a single source of
truth.
• Strengthen internal controls, approval workflows, and audit readiness.
• Enable effective cash flow management, budgeting, and financial planning.
• Support multi-branch, multi-currency, and tax-compliant business operations.
• Provide implementation teams and stakeholders with a clear roadmap for ERP configuration
and deployment.
This document serves as a reference manual for management, finance teams, auditors, ERP
administrators, implementation consultants, and operational stakeholders involved in the
planning, execution, and maintenance of the organization’s financial management processes.
By adhering to the principles and procedures outlined herein, organizations can achieve greater
financial visibility, improved decision-making, operational efficiency, and sustainable business
growth.
The procedures and guidelines described in this document should be reviewed periodically and
updated as business requirements, regulatory frameworks, and organizational structures evolve.

1.	 ACCOUNTING & FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT
The Accounting & Financial Management System is the core financial intelligence and control platform
of a multinational organization. It is designed to manage high-volume, multi-currency, multi-country
trading operations involving procurement, inventory movement, warehousing, sales distribution, logistics
costs, and inter-company transactions.
Unlike traditional accounting systems, this module is transaction-driven, inventory-integrated, and
profitability-focused, enabling real-time financial visibility at product, customer, warehouse, branch, and
country levels.
It ensures that every operational activity automatically translates into accurate financial impact,
providing end-to-end financial governance across the entire supply chain.
1.1.	System Design Philosophy
The system is built on five core principles:
1.1.1  Transaction-Driven Accounting
Every operational action generates automatic accounting entries:
Purchase -> Inventory + Payable + Duty
Sales > Revenue + Receivable + COGS (COGS represents the actual cost of the parts that are sold, not
the selling price.)
Stock Transfer -> Inter-warehouse financial adjustment
Logistics Cost -> Cost allocation to inventory or expense

1.2.	Chart of Accounts (COA)
The Chart of Accounts in this ERP system is not just a static financial list — it is a dynamic financial
control framework designed for global spare parts trading operations.
It ensures that every financial transaction is correctly classified, automatically posted, and accurately

decision-making capability.

1.3.	General Ledger (GL)
The General Ledger (GL) serves as the central financial repository of the ERP system, where all approved
financial transactions are recorded and maintained. It acts as the primary source for financial reporting,
audit compliance, management analysis, and statutory reporting.
The GL is designed to provide complete financial visibility across multiple companies, countries, branches,
warehouses, departments, projects, and cost centers while maintaining strict financial controls and audit
integrity.
1.3.1  Core Functionality
•
Real-time financial transaction processing
•
Double-entry accounting system
•
Manual journal entry management
•
Recurring journal entries
•
Adjustment and accrual entries
•
Reversal and correction entries
•
Multi-company and multi-branch accounting
•
Multi-currency accounting support
•
Cost center and department allocation
•
Project-based financial tracking
•
Complete audit trail and transaction history
1.3.2  Financial Posting Control
To ensure financial accuracy and compliance, operational transactions generated by Procurement, Sales,
Inventory, Payroll, Asset Management, and other ERP modules are first recorded as source transactions
within their respective modules.
Financial postings to the General Ledger are performed only after validation and approval by authorized
finance personnel.
