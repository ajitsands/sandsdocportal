PREFACE  
This document describes the workflow and functional processes related to the 
Purchase and Vendor Management System. It has been prepared based on the 
discussions held with the management and operational teams, with the objective 
of clearly defining the purchase procedures, data flow, approval hierarchy, and 
standard operating practices.
The document outlines the end-to-end process, starting from branch requirements 
and stock shortages, through the purchase request process, vendor quotation 
management, purchase order generation, verification procedures, and final stock 
updates. It also explains the roles and responsibilities of each department involved, 
including the Branches, Main Store, Purchase Department, and Management.
This document primarily concentrates on the workflow of the Purchase Department 
and its integration with the Main Store and Branch operations. While this document 
defines the purchase-related processes and associated data flow in detail, the 
complete branch-level operational workflow is described separately in the next 
document Doc-003 v-1. The objective here is to clearly establish how purchase 
activities are initiated, processed, approved, executed, and recorded within the 
system.
In addition to the functional workflow, this document includes key management 
control points, vendor evaluation logic, approval stages, audit tracking mechanisms, 
and structured data flow processes required to ensure accuracy, transparency, 
compliance, and accountability in procurement activities.
This preface serves as an introduction to the overall Purchase and Vendor 
Management framework. The document should be used as a reference for system 
development, implementation planning, operational execution, and standardization 
across departments. The contents of this document are based on the requirements 
and discussions conducted during the analysis phase and are intended to guide 
both technical development and operational usage of the system.

1. Vendor Master Management
Popular Auto Spare and A/C Parts Co.  operates as a multi-country, multi-branch enterprise; 
however, the fundamental operating principle is that all procurement activities are centrally controlled 
from a designated headquarters location. This centralized purchasing model ensures uniform pricing 
strategy, supplier governance, negotiation control, and compliance consistency across all branches and 
countries.
Vendors from different countries will be registered within a unified global Vendor Master. Each supplier 
record will capture country-specific regulatory, tax, currency, and banking details while remaining 
governed under centralized approval authority. This structure allows the company to maintain a single 
source of truth for supplier data while supporting international procurement operations.
Vendor master records include the following key parameters:
 • Vendor name
 • Vendor code 
 • Legal entity name
 • Contact person details
 • Phone and email information
 • Registered address
 • Country and operating region
 • Tax/VAT registration details
 • Payment terms
 • Currency preference
 • Bank and remittance details
 • Supplier category (OEM / Aftermarket / Local / International)
 • Brand association (if applicable)
 • Credit limit and risk classification
 • Status (Active / On Hold / Inactive)

understanding of local sales behaviour and fast-moving products, they are authorized to set thresholds

control.

items that either do not have predefined reorder levels or whose existing configuration is insufficient 
compared to actual usage trends.

4. Less Item List Handling
Current Process
In the existing environment, the less item list is generated manually and handled through paper-based 
and fragmented workflows:
 • The less item list will be received from the branches/store through email.
 • The purchase team will print the less item list and handed over to the store team.
 • Store staff physically verify each item against available stock and manually mark quantities on 
paper.
 • The purchase team re-enters the entire list into the system.
 • Purchase officers cross-check past transactions and purchase history item-by-item before raising 
RFQ.
 • Searching historical transactions in the current system is slow and difficult, especially for previous 
accounting years.
 • System performance issues and loading delays further increase processing time.
 • Final RFQs are prepared and sent to suppliers by the Purchase Team or CTO.
This process is labor-intensive, error-prone, and heavily dependent on manual verification and duplicate 
data entry.
Proposed-ERP-Workflow
In the proposed ERP workflow, once branch managers confirm their individual Less Item Lists, the 
system automatically generates a consolidated multi-branch view for centralized store verification. This 
ensures that replenishment decisions are made based on complete organizational visibility rather than 
isolated branch requests.
Consolidated View Structure
The Store Manager receives a structured dashboard that aggregates confirmed requests from all 
branches in a single matrix-style report. The report includes:
 • Item details
 • Branch-wise required quantity
 • Branch-wise available quantity
 • Central store available quantity

10. Data Flow Process for Purchase
Overview
This process explains how a stock request originating from a branch is handled through the main store, 
purchase department, CTO approval, vendor quotation, purchase order generation, goods receipt, and 
final stock update. The objective of this process is to ensure accurate procurement, controlled approvals, 
and proper stock visibility across all branches.
10.1. Step 1: Branch Requirement to Main Store
When a branch identifies that certain items are running low or out of stock, the branch manager creates 
a stock request in the system.
This request is automatically sent to the Main Store.
The request includes: (Details in the Page No : 8)
 • Branch name
 • Item details
 • Required quantities
 • Request date
 • Requesting user
This ensures that all stock movements begin from a centralized process.
10.2. Step 2: Main Store Availability Check 
Once the request reaches the Main Store, the Store Manager reviews the request and checks whether 
the requested items are available in the main store inventory.
If items are available:
1. The Store Manager assigns the request to a Store Keeper.
2. The Store Keeper collects the requested items.
3. The items are placed in the designated branch pickup area.
4. The system generates a Store Delivery Note for that branch.
5. The branch is notified that the items are ready for pickup.
This completes the process for items that are available in the main store. (Detailed Explanation in Doc-
003 V1.0))

1. The Store Manager creates a Store Purchase Request.
