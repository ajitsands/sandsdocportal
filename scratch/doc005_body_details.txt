1.	 ACCOUNTING & FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT
The Accounting & Financial Management System is the core financial intelligence and control platform
of a multinational organization. It is designed to manage high-volume, multi-currency, multi-country
trading operations involving procurement, inventory movement, warehousing, sales distribution, logistics
costs, and inter-company transactions.
Unlike traditional accounting systems, this module is transaction-driven, inventory-integrated, and
profitability-focused, enabling real-time financial visibility at product, customer, warehouse, branch, and
country levels.
It ensures that every operational activity automatically translates into accurate financial impact,
providing end-to-end financial governance across the entire supply chain.
1.1.	System Design Philosophy
The system is built on five core principles:
1.1.1  Transaction-Driven Accounting
Every operational action generates automatic accounting entries:
Purchase -> Inventory + Payable + Duty
Sales > Revenue + Receivable + COGS (COGS represents the actual cost of the parts that are sold, not
the selling price.)
Stock Transfer -> Inter-warehouse financial adjustment
Logistics Cost -> Cost allocation to inventory or expense

1.3.	General Ledger (GL)
The General Ledger (GL) serves as the central financial repository of the ERP system, where all approved
financial transactions are recorded and maintained. It acts as the primary source for financial reporting,
audit compliance, management analysis, and statutory reporting.
The GL is designed to provide complete financial visibility across multiple companies, countries, branches,
warehouses, departments, projects, and cost centers while maintaining strict financial controls and audit
integrity.

1.3.2  Financial Posting Control
To ensure financial accuracy and compliance, operational transactions generated by Procurement, Sales,
Inventory, Payroll, Asset Management, and other ERP modules are first recorded as source transactions
within their respective modules.
Financial postings to the General Ledger are performed only after validation and approval by authorized
finance personnel.
This approach ensures:

2.	 Inventory-Integrated Accounting
Inventory represents one of the most significant financial assets in the organization. The Inventory-
Integrated Accounting module provides a structured framework for inventory valuation, stock accounting,
cost control, and profitability analysis while ensuring strict financial governance.
The system maintains synchronization between inventory quantities and financial records, enabling
management to accurately assess stock value, cost of sales, inventory turnover, and business profitability.
Unlike fully automated inventory accounting systems, financial valuation entries are subject to review
and validation by the Finance Department to ensure accuracy, compliance, and proper cost allocation.
2.1.	Inventory Accounting Logic
Every inventory movement has a financial impact that must be reflected in the organization’s accounting
records.
Inventory Transaction- ------------------ Financial Impact
Purchase Receipt------------------------- Inventory Quantity Increase
Sales Issue------------------------------- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) Recognition
Stock Transfer---------------------------- Inter-Warehouse Inventory Adjustment
Stock Adjustment------------------------ Inventory Gain/Loss Recognition
Stock Return----------------------------- Inventory Value Adjustment
Damaged or Obsolete Stock------------- Inventory Provision or Write-Off
The system records all inventory movements and maintains complete transaction history for audit and
valuation purposes.
