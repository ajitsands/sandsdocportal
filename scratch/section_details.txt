1.1.	Objective
The Sales Module is a core component of the ERP system designed to manage and optimize the complete
sales lifecycle across all branches of Popular Auto Spare Parts Company. The module is intended to
support high-speed operations in a spare parts environment, where accuracy, speed of identification,
and pricing control are critical.
Each branch operates semi-independently with its own inventory, sales team, and customer interactions.
However, all branches follow standardized processes governed by centralized controls such as pricing
policies, approval workflows, and promotional rules. The system ensures seamless coordination between
branches, management, and customers while maintaining real-time data synchronization.
This document defines the combined Business Requirements and Functional Specifications for
the Sales Module of the ERP system. The module is designed to support and streamline the complete
sales lifecycle across multiple branches of the organization, ensuring operational efficiency, pricing
control, inventory accuracy, and enhanced customer service.
The system must support high transaction volumes, rapid item identification, and flexible sales workflows
while maintaining strict governance over pricing, approvals, and financial accounting.
1.2.	Business Overview and Operational Context
The organization operates as a geographically distributed enterprise with a multi-country and multi-
branch structure, where each branch functions as an independent operational unit while remaining
fully integrated within a centralized ERP ecosystem.
Each branch is established within a specific country and operates under that country’s regulatory, taxation,
and currency requirements. At the same time, all branches adhere to standardized organizational policies
related to inventory control, sales processes, and reporting. This ensures consistency in operations while
allowing flexibility to address local market conditions.
Every branch is managed by a Branch Manager and supported by a team of sales personnel. Each
branch maintains its own inventory, customer base, and day-to-day sales operations. However, data
across all branches and countries is synchronized in real time to provide centralized visibility and control
for management.
Stock is transferred to branches either from a central warehouse, regional distribution centres, or
through local procurement processes. Upon receipt, the Branch Manager is responsible for verifying the
stock and recording it into the branch inventory system.
Within each branch, items are organized according to a predefined location structure, which is designed
to optimize storage efficiency and retrieval speed. Sales personnel are responsible for placing items
in their designated locations and confirming placement using P-Code scanning. This ensures accurate
tracking of item locations and supports quick identification during sales transactions.

1.7.	Sales Attribution & Performance Accountability (Cart-to-Billing Ownership)
In a dynamic branch environment, especially during peak hours, multiple salespersons may collaborate
in serving a single customer by identifying items and adding them to the cart. However, for the purposes of
accountability, performance tracking, and incentive calculation, it is essential to clearly define ownership
of each completed sale.
The system shall adopt a “Cart-to-Billing Ownership” concept, where the final responsibility and
credit for a sale are assigned to the salesperson who completes the billing process.
This means that regardless of how many salespersons contributed to building the cart (through item
identification, scanning, or cart transfer), the sale will be officially recorded under the salesperson who
pushes the cart to invoice generation.
This approach ensures:
•
Clear ownership of sales transactions
•
Accurate performance measurement of sales personnel
•
Simplified commission calculation
•
Elimination of ambiguity in shared sales scenarios
Additionally, for internal analysis and operational transparency, the system may optionally track
contributing salespersons without affecting final sales ownership.

1.8.	Sales Return Management Module
The Sales Return module manages the return of goods from customers after either:
• A Sales Invoice has been generated, or
• A Delivery Note (DN) has been issued (with or without subsequent invoicing).
Given the operational nature of the business, where delivery may occur before invoicing, the system
supports a unified return mechanism capable of handling both document types seamlessly.
In addition, the system incorporates a Global Return Policy configured and controlled by the CPO /
Purchase Department. This policy enables authorized users to define a standard return window (such as
7 days or 10 days) applicable across all items and transactions.
Under this global policy:
Returns are permitted only within the defined time period from the date of invoice or delivery.
The system automatically validates return eligibility based on the configured duration.
Any return request exceeding the allowed period will be restricted and blocked by the system.
Furthermore, the system provides the flexibility to define Item-Level Return Policies. The CPO / Purchase
Department can configure specific return rules for individual items, overriding the global policy where
required. This allows better control for sensitive, non-returnable, or special-category items.
Additionally, an Override Mechanism with Manager Approval is available:
Authorized managers can approve return requests even after the allowed return period has expired.
The duration within which such overrides are permitted (e.g., additional grace days) can be predefined
and controlled by the CPO / Purchase Department.
All override actions are subject to proper authorization and system tracking to ensure accountability
and audit compliance.
This multi-level return policy framework ensures strong governance while maintaining operational
flexibility, enabling the organization to handle returns efficiently without compromising control.

1.1.	Objective
The Sales Module is a core component of the ERP system designed to manage and optimize the complete
sales lifecycle across all branches of Popular Auto Spare Parts Company. The module is intended to
support high-speed operations in a spare parts environment, where accuracy, speed of identification,
and pricing control are critical.
Each branch operates semi-independently with its own inventory, sales team, and customer interactions.
However, all branches follow standardized processes governed by centralized controls such as pricing
policies, approval workflows, and promotional rules. The system ensures seamless coordination between
branches, management, and customers while maintaining real-time data synchronization.
This document defines the combined Business Requirements and Functional Specifications for
the Sales Module of the ERP system. The module is designed to support and streamline the complete
sales lifecycle across multiple branches of the organization, ensuring operational efficiency, pricing
control, inventory accuracy, and enhanced customer service.
The system must support high transaction volumes, rapid item identification, and flexible sales workflows
while maintaining strict governance over pricing, approvals, and financial accounting.
1.2.	Business Overview and Operational Context
The organization operates as a geographically distributed enterprise with a multi-country and multi-
branch structure, where each branch functions as an independent operational unit while remaining
fully integrated within a centralized ERP ecosystem.
Each branch is established within a specific country and operates under that country’s regulatory, taxation,
and currency requirements. At the same time, all branches adhere to standardized organizational policies
related to inventory control, sales processes, and reporting. This ensures consistency in operations while
allowing flexibility to address local market conditions.
Every branch is managed by a Branch Manager and supported by a team of sales personnel. Each
branch maintains its own inventory, customer base, and day-to-day sales operations. However, data
across all branches and countries is synchronized in real time to provide centralized visibility and control
for management.
Stock is transferred to branches either from a central warehouse, regional distribution centres, or
through local procurement processes. Upon receipt, the Branch Manager is responsible for verifying the
stock and recording it into the branch inventory system.
Within each branch, items are organized according to a predefined location structure, which is designed
to optimize storage efficiency and retrieval speed. Sales personnel are responsible for placing items
in their designated locations and confirming placement using P-Code scanning. This ensures accurate
tracking of item locations and supports quick identification during sales transactions.
