PREFACE
The Sales Counter Process is a critical component of the overall business 
operation, ensuring smooth, accurate, and accountable handling of day-to-day 
sales activities. This document outlines the standardized procedures for managing 
sales transactions, return handling, sales posting, and inventory closing, forming a 
structured workflow that enhances operational efficiency and financial transparency.
At the sales counter level, every transaction—whether a sale or a return—directly 
impacts inventory levels, financial records, and customer satisfaction. Therefore, 
it is essential to maintain a controlled and well-defined process. The system is 
designed to ensure that all sales are recorded accurately, returns are processed 
based on defined policies such as “Return Allowed,” and unauthorized actions are 
prevented through system validations.
Sales posting plays a vital role in transferring transactional data into the 
accounting system, ensuring that revenue, taxes, and receivables are properly 
recorded. Similarly, inventory closing ensures that stock movements are validated, 
reconciled, and locked for a specific period, preventing discrepancies and 
unauthorized modifications.
This document aims to provide clear guidance to users and administrators on how 
to effectively operate the sales counter system while maintaining data integrity, 
accountability, and compliance with business rules. By following these processes, 
organizations can achieve better control over sales operations, minimize errors, 
and ensure accurate financial and inventory reporting.

1.	 Sales
1.1.	Objective
The Sales Module is a core component of the ERP system designed to manage and optimize the complete 
sales lifecycle across all branches of Popular Auto Spare Parts Company. The module is intended to 
support high-speed operations in a spare parts environment, where accuracy, speed of identification, 
and pricing control are critical.
Each branch operates semi-independently with its own inventory, sales team, and customer interactions. 
However, all branches follow standardized processes governed by centralized controls such as pricing 
policies, approval workflows, and promotional rules. The system ensures seamless coordination between 
branches, management, and customers while maintaining real-time data synchronization.
This document defines the combined Business Requirements and Functional Specifications for 
the Sales Module of the ERP system. The module is designed to support and streamline the complete 
sales lifecycle across multiple branches of the organization, ensuring operational efficiency, pricing 
control, inventory accuracy, and enhanced customer service.
The system must support high transaction volumes, rapid item identification, and flexible sales workflows 
while maintaining strict governance over pricing, approvals, and financial accounting.
1.2.	Business Overview and Operational Context
The organization operates as a geographically distributed enterprise with a multi-country and multi-
branch structure, where each branch functions as an independent operational unit while remaining 
fully integrated within a centralized ERP ecosystem.
Each branch is established within a specific country and operates under that country’s regulatory, taxation, 
and currency requirements. At the same time, all branches adhere to standardized organizational policies 
related to inventory control, sales processes, and reporting. This ensures consistency in operations while 
allowing flexibility to address local market conditions.
Every branch is managed by a Branch Manager and supported by a team of sales personnel. Each 
branch maintains its own inventory, customer base, and day-to-day sales operations. However, data 
across all branches and countries is synchronized in real time to provide centralized visibility and control 
for management.
Stock is transferred to branches either from a central warehouse, regional distribution centres, or 
through local procurement processes. Upon receipt, the Branch Manager is responsible for verifying the 
stock and recording it into the branch inventory system.
Within each branch, items are organized according to a predefined location structure, which is designed 
to optimize storage efficiency and retrieval speed. Sales personnel are responsible for placing items 
in their designated locations and confirming placement using P-Code scanning. This ensures accurate 
tracking of item locations and supports quick identification during sales transactions.

1.3.	Customer Master Management
The Customer Master module serves as the centralized repository for maintaining all customer-related 
information across the organization. It is a critical component that supports sales operations, credit 
management, and financial tracking across multiple branches.
Given the organization operates in a multi-branch and multi-location environment, the system is 
designed to ensure that customer data is managed centrally while still allowing branches the flexibility 
to perform day-to-day operations efficiently. This approach enables consistency, prevents duplication, 
and ensures that financial exposure and customer relationships are managed at an organizational level 
rather than in isolation.
The module is tightly integrated with key business functions including sales, accounting, credit control, 
and reporting, thereby enabling a seamless flow of information across the system.
1.3.1  Business Objectives
The primary objective of the Customer Master module is to establish a single source of truth for customer 
information across all branches. This ensures that all customer interactions, transactions, and financial 
records are consistently maintained and easily accessible.
The system is designed to:
	•
Maintain centralized and standardized customer data 
	•
Prevent duplicate customer creation across branches 
	•
Enable effective credit management and control 
	•
Support multiple customer categories with different business rules 
	•
Provide consolidated visibility of customer transactions and outstanding balances 
	•
Allow controlled access for branches to perform customer-related operations 
The system supports both globally shared customers and, where required, branch-specific customer 
records. All customer records remain accessible across branches, while the system identifies and 
maintains the originating branch as the primary branch for each customer.
1.3.2  Customer Classification
Customers are categorized based on their nature of business and transactional behaviour. This 
classification allows the system to apply appropriate pricing policies, credit rules, and operational 
workflows.

Customer types include retail customers, corporate clients, company, garages, dealers, and credit 
customers. Each category is associated with specific characteristics such as transaction volume, pricing 
agreements, and credit eligibility.
For example, retail customers typically operate on a cash basis with minimal data requirements, 
whereas corporate and company customers may have defined credit limits, negotiated pricing, and 
formal contractual arrangements. Garage and dealer customers often engage in frequent transactions 
and may benefit from specialized pricing structures or discounts.
This classification ensures that the system can adapt dynamically to different customer profiles and 
business requirements.
1.3.3  Customer Information Management
The system captures comprehensive customer information to support operational, financial, and 
compliance requirements.
At a basic level, each customer record includes a unique system-generated customer code, 
customer name, type, status, and primary branch association. Customers can also be categorized 
into segments to support reporting and analysis.
Detailed contact information is maintained, including contact person details, mobile numbers, phone 
numbers, email addresses, and preferred communication methods and multipul attachments. 
This ensures effective communication and relationship management.
The system supports multiple addresses for each customer, including billing and shipping addresses, 
along with location details such as city and country. 
To meet regulatory and compliance requirements, the system also captures legal identifiers such as 
commercial registration numbers, VAT or tax numbers, and other relevant documentation 
depending on the customer type.
Banking details are maintained for customers where applicable, particularly for corporate and credit 
customers. These details include bank name, account holder name, account number or IBAN, 
SWIFT code, and branch information, supporting financial transactions such as refunds and 
settlements.
1.3.4  Credit Management
A key aspect of the Customer Master module is credit control. For customers operating on credit, the 
system maintains detailed credit-related parameters including credit limit, credit period, current 
outstanding balance, and allowable credit variation.
Before processing any credit transaction, the system validates the customer’s available credit. If the 
transaction exceeds the defined limit, the system generates warnings and restricts further processing 
based on configured rules.
In situations where exceptions are required, the system supports a structured approval workflow 
for credit overrides. Requests for exceeding the defined credit limits can be routed to authorized 
personnel such as the Branch Manager, Chief Purchase Officer (CPO), or Accounts Department.

1.3.5  Customer Creation and Data Control
To maintain data integrity, customer creation is governed by strict validation and control mechanisms. 
Customers can be created either directly from the Customer Master module or during the sales process.
The system performs duplicate checks based on key identifiers such as mobile number, commercial 
registration number, and customer name to prevent redundancy. For certain customer types, 
especially those involving credit or corporate relationships, the system may enforce an approval workflow 
before the customer record becomes active.
This ensures that customer data remains accurate, consistent, and reliable across the organization.
1.3.6  Multi-Branch Customer Handling
In a multi-branch setup, it is common for customers to transact with more than one branch. The system 
is designed to handle this efficiently through a centralized customer model.
Under this model, a single customer record is shared across all branches. While transactions are recorded 
at the branch level, the system maintains both branch-wise transaction history and consolidated financial 
exposure.
Credit limits are enforced at a global level. For example, if a customer has a credit limit of 10,000 and 
has already utilized 6,000 in one branch, only the remaining 4,000 is available for transactions across 
other branches. This ensures unified control over credit exposure.
Branches are allowed to access and use customer records for transactions, but modification rights can 
be restricted based on user roles and permissions, ensuring centralized governance.
1.3.7  Customer Financial Tracking
The system maintains a comprehensive financial history for each customer through an integrated ledger. 
This includes all invoices, payments, returns, and adjustments.
Users can view outstanding invoices, overdue amounts, and overall account balances. The system 
supports flexible payment allocation, allowing settlements against specific invoices or as on-account 
payments.
This level of financial visibility enables better credit control, faster collections, and improved customer 
relationship management.

1.3.8  Customer Settlement Management
The Customer Settlement Management process within the Sales Module governs the reconciliation of all 
financial transactions between the organization and its customers, ensuring that outstanding balances, 
payments, credit sales, and adjustments are accurately tracked and settled in a controlled and auditable 
manner. The system maintains a running ledger for each customer that reflects invoices, cash memos, 
credit notes, returns, payments received, and any other financial adjustments. It enables branches 
and the accounts department to monitor customer outstanding balances, credit utilization, and aging 
analysis in real time across a multi-branch environment. Settlement activities may include recording 
payments against specific invoices or as general receipts, adjusting advances, applying credit notes, and 
reconciling discrepancies between expected and received amounts.
From a control perspective, the system shall enforce validation of customer credit limits, payment terms, 
and outstanding balances before allowing further credit-based transactions. Any settlement involving 
adjustments, write-offs, or exceptions shall be routed through an approval workflow based on predefined 
thresholds and authorization levels, ensuring proper oversight by branch managers or the accounts 
department. The system shall maintain a complete audit trail of all settlement activities, including the 
initiating user, approving authority, timestamps, and remarks, ensuring full traceability and compliance.
Additionally, the Customer Settlement Management process integrates closely with accounting and 
reporting modules to automatically generate the necessary journal entries for payments, receivables 
adjustments, and reconciliations. It also supports branch-level and centralized (head office) visibility, 
enabling consolidated financial monitoring as well as branch-specific settlement tracking. Overall, this 
module ensures accurate financial reconciliation, strengthens credit control, reduces outstanding risks, 
and provides clear visibility into customer financial positions across the organization.
1.3.9  Approval and Governance
To ensure proper control and governance, the system incorporates approval mechanisms for critical 
customer-related actions. These include changes to credit limits, customer blocking or unblocking, and 
eligibility for special pricing.
Such actions require authorization from designated roles such as Branch Managers, the CPO, or the 
Accounts Department. This layered approval structure ensures accountability and reduces the risk of 
unauthorized decisions.
The implementation of a centralized Customer Master module enables the organization to achieve 
consistent and reliable customer data management across all branches. It strengthens credit control, 
enhances operational efficiency, and improves financial visibility.
By eliminating duplication, enforcing standardized processes, and integrating with core business 
functions, the system supports better decision-making and contributes to improved customer service 
and organizational performance.

1.5.	Item Identification, Intelligent Search & Pricing Control
Accurate and efficient item identification is a critical success factor in the spare parts sales process. 
Due to the nature of the business, customers often provide incomplete or indirect information such as 
vehicle model, VIN/chassis number, or physical samples instead of exact part numbers. This makes the 
identification process highly dependent on both salesperson expertise and system intelligence.
To address this challenge, the system is designed to support multiple identification methods and provide 
a highly responsive and intelligent search mechanism. The objective is to enable salespersons to quickly 
and accurately identify the required parts, suggest alternatives where necessary, and proceed with the 
sales process without delays.
In addition to identification, the system must support real-time access to relevant transactional 
and pricing data. This ensures that salespersons can make informed decisions while interacting with 
customers, especially in competitive or negotiation scenarios.

1.8.	Sales Return Management Module
The Sales Return module manages the return of goods from customers after either:
	• A Sales Invoice has been generated, or
	• A Delivery Note (DN) has been issued (with or without subsequent invoicing).
Given the operational nature of the business, where delivery may occur before invoicing, the system 
supports a unified return mechanism capable of handling both document types seamlessly.
In addition, the system incorporates a Global Return Policy configured and controlled by the CPO / 
Purchase Department. This policy enables authorized users to define a standard return window (such as 
7 days or 10 days) applicable across all items and transactions.
Under this global policy:
Returns are permitted only within the defined time period from the date of invoice or delivery.
The system automatically validates return eligibility based on the configured duration.
Any return request exceeding the allowed period will be restricted and blocked by the system.
Furthermore, the system provides the flexibility to define Item-Level Return Policies. The CPO / Purchase 
Department can configure specific return rules for individual items, overriding the global policy where 
required. This allows better control for sensitive, non-returnable, or special-category items.
Additionally, an Override Mechanism with Manager Approval is available:
Authorized managers can approve return requests even after the allowed return period has expired.
The duration within which such overrides are permitted (e.g., additional grace days) can be predefined 
and controlled by the CPO / Purchase Department.
All override actions are subject to proper authorization and system tracking to ensure accountability 
and audit compliance.
This multi-level return policy framework ensures strong governance while maintaining operational 
flexibility, enabling the organization to handle returns efficiently without compromising control.

1.19.	Audit & Control
The Sales Module incorporates a comprehensive audit and control framework to ensure full transparency, 
accountability, and compliance across all branches, transactions, and device-based operations. Every 
activity—covering quotations, delivery notes, invoices, cash memos, returns, less item requests, and 
accounting entries—is systematically logged with user identity, timestamps, branch details, and status 
changes, enabling complete traceability throughout the transaction lifecycle. The system enforces 
strict data integrity by preventing deletion of posted or finalized documents and instead supports 
controlled cancellations and reversal entries with mandatory reasons and appropriate authorization. 
Sensitive operations such as pricing overrides, credit limit exceptions, discounts, and return processing 
are governed through structured approval workflows involving authorized personnel such as Branch 
Managers, Chief Purchase Officer (CPO), and Accounts Department, with full tracking of approvals, 
rejections, and remarks.
In addition, the system extends audit and control mechanisms to handheld devices used by sales 
personnel. All actions performed via handheld devices—such as item scanning, cart creation, cart 
transfers/share, item additions/removals, and final billing—are tracked with device identifiers, user login 
details, time stamps, and location/branch association. The system ensures that transactions initiated 
on handheld devices are securely synchronized with the central system in real time or near real time, 
maintaining consistency across inventory, sales, and accounting records. Any discrepancies, overrides, 
or offline activities (if supported) are logged and reconciled upon synchronization. The concept of cart 
ownership ensures that the salesperson who finalizes the transaction is accountable for the sale, enabling 
accurate sales performance tracking and commission calculation.
Overall, the audit framework ensures end-to-end traceability of all transactions across both system and 
handheld interfaces, maintains strong controls over financial and operational activities, and provides 
complete visibility to management and accounts teams in a multi-branch environment. This ensures 
data integrity, prevents unauthorized manipulation, supports compliance with accounting standards, and 
enables reliable monitoring, reporting, and decision-making across the organization.

1.20.	 Pricing Update Notification

are effectively communicated and consistently applied across all branches, users, and devices, including 
handheld devices. When pricing updates are made—whether due to cost changes, supplier revisions, 
promotional adjustments, or management decisions—the system shall capture these updates centrally
